The copper tang of old battery acid coated Ines’s tongue. She wiped her mouth with a grease-unfriendly sleeve, her eyes fixed on the oscillating green line of the shortwave receiver. Outside, the sky remained a bruised violet, a permanent twilight trapped beneath heavy, suffocating layers of silicate grit. This atmospheric dust, a thick shroud of planetary debris, settled over the artificial atoll like marine snow drifting through the bathypelagic zone. It choked the sun and turned the ocean into a mirror of lead, a dead expanse where light went to die. The station, a skeletal structure of rusted iron and polymer, groaned under the weight of the settling grime.

The static hissed, a rhythmic rasp against the silence of the station. Then, a break. A pattern emerged from the white noise: dot-dash-dot-dot. Her fingers froze over the brass dials. It was the unmistakable signature of the Aethelgard, a freighter lost to the crushing pressure of the deep in 2107. Every nautical chart in her archives listed the vessel at the bottom of the Trench, long since reclaimed by the silt. Yet, the signal continued, rhythmic and insistent, pulling itself from the suffocating void of the dead communications bands.

When the coastal cities first succumbed to the rising, acid-etched tides, Ines sought refuge here. She had traded the study of currents for the stewardship of this lonely light. The solitude of the platform offered a heavy sanctuary where the chaos of the dying mainland could not reach her.

The signal pulsed again, more frequent now. Each burst of Morse code felt like a sudden bioluminescent flash in a lightless trench, startling and impossible. Ines leaned closer to the speaker, her breath fogging the glass of the monitor. The frequency was too low for any modern vessel, too ancient, too much like a heartbeat from a corpse. She checked the radar; the screen showed nothing but the empty, churning waves of the artificial sea. No metal hull disturbed the surface. No heat signature broke the gloom. The ocean remained a blank, unblinking eye of darkness.

She adjusted the gain, her knuckles white against the metal casing. The code shifted, becoming a frantic, jagged string of characters. Her mind raced through the logs of the 2107 disaster, searching for a logic that did not exist. The Aethelgard was a memory, a tragedy etched into the seabed. Now, it was a voice, calling from a grave of salt.

The radio emitted a sharp, piercing squeal, then settled into a low, rhythmic thrum. Ines stood, her joints stiff from the damp air. She stepped toward the heavy reinforced window. Far below, where the artificial foundations met the black water, a single, pale light flickered beneath the waves. It moved with a strange, undulating motion, rising toward the surface, or perhaps descending into the abyss. She pressed her forehead against the cold pane, watching the light dissolve into the swirling grey silt, leaving the immense, swallowing dark.
