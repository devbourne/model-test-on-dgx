The lens felt cool and smooth beneath Ines’s fingertips, like the shell of a deep-sea mollusk, the only thing not blurred by the perpetual twilight. Outside the thick glass, the artificial island’s perimeter lights bled into the bruised sky, swallowed by the endless dust. Her knuckles were white on the railing as the Morse signal pulsed through the static: *dah-dah-dah-dah… dah-dah-dah-dah…* A rhythm from the *Aurora*, sunk forty years ago in the Pacific storm that took her husband. She’d chosen this lighthouse to keep his memory afloat, to watch the sea where he vanished, a lonely vigil against the encroaching gloom.

The dust, thick as plankton in a stagnant tide, coated everything – the metal walkways, the algae-slicked rocks below, even the air she breathed. It had settled after the Great Dusting, turning day into a slow, grey decay. Ines remembered the *Aurora*’s last transmission, crackling with salt and panic, then silence. She’d been the oceanographer on the research vessel *Nereus*, monitoring currents, when the call came. She’d never left the sea, not truly, not after he was gone. This island, built for light in a world that no longer needed it, was her only anchor.

The signal repeated, clear as a conch shell held to the ear. *Dah-dah-dah-dah…* She traced the rhythm on the cold console, her breath fogging the screen. The *Aurora*’s code. Impossible. Her hands trembled as she keyed the response, the old Morse code a muscle memory long dormant. *Dah-dah-dah…* She sent her confirmation, a single, shaky pulse into the static. Silence. Then, the signal returned, stronger, closer. *Dah-dah-dah-dah… dah-dah-dah-dah…* It wasn’t a ghost. It was a ship. *Her* ship. The one that shouldn’t exist.

She stared at the water, the dark waves rolling in slow, heavy motions like the slow pulse of a deep-sea squid. The signal faded, replaced by the low thrum of the lighthouse generator, a sound as constant as the dust. She turned back to the console, the screen dark now, the only sound the rhythmic sigh of the sea against the island’s concrete base. The dust swirled in the weak light, thick as dead plankton, obscuring the horizon. She pressed her palm flat against the cold glass, feeling the faint vibration of the island’s deep hum. Somewhere in the grey water, the *Aurora*’s ghost might be real, or the dust might finally be playing tricks on her tired eyes. She stood there, the only living thing on the island, listening to the sea hold its breath.
